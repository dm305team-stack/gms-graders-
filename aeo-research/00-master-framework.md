## The Core Insight

Generative engines do not rank pages the way traditional search engines do. They:

1. Retrieve content via dense vector embeddings, not keyword matches
2. Chunk and re-rank content via Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
3. Build evidence graphs and resolve contradictions before citing
4. Reward novel information and penalize redundancy
5. Validate authority through off-site consensus, not just backlinks

### The SEO/AEO Disconnect (Empirical Baseline)

- Only **12%** of domains cited in AI answers overlap with Google's top 10 organic results for the same query
- **80%** of LLM citations come from pages that do not rank in Google's top 100
- The top 10% of pages most frequently cited by major LLMs actually have less organic traffic, fewer ranking keywords, and fewer backlinks than the bottom 90% of cited pages

This proves AEO is a distinct discipline from SEO. A site can rank #1 organically and remain invisible in AI answers.

## The Unified Scoring System

The auditor combines two complementary frameworks:

- **GEO-16**: Mathematical scoring with 16 pillars and operational thresholds
- **CITABLE**: Operational checklist for content engineering and recommendations

### GEO-16 Mathematical Thresholds

Source: arXiv:2509.10762 (UC Berkeley / Wrodium Research, September 2025). Analyzed 1,702 citations across Brave Summary, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity using 1,100 URLs and 70 product-intent prompts.

Definitions:
- **G**: Page Quality Score, normalized to the range [0, 1]
- **H**: Count of the 16 pillars where the page registers a positive hit

Operational citation threshold:
- **G ≥ 0.70 AND H ≥ 12** unlocks a **78% cross-engine citation rate**
- Page quality odds ratio as citation predictor: **4.2** (95% CI [3.1, 5.7])

The 16 pillars cluster into three predictive categories:

1. **Structured Data and Semantic HTML**: JSON-LD schemas, hierarchical H1/H2/H3, tables, lists
2. **Metadata and Freshness**: `<time>` tags, last-updated stamps, version notes
3. **Provenance, Authority, and Trust**: External linking profiles, verifiable claims, source trails, conflict-of-interest disclosures

### CITABLE Operational Checklist

Each letter is an audit dimension applied per URL. This is the actionable layer where GEO-16 gaps translate into client-facing recommendations.

| Letter | Dimension | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| C | Clear Entity and Structure | First 100 words act as a standalone micro-answer (BLUF format) |
| I | Intent Architecture | 5-7 H2/H3 headings covering query fan-out variants |
| T | Third-Party Validation | Presence on Wikipedia, G2, Reddit, industry forums |
| A | Answer Grounding | Standalone, verifiable facts with inline citations |
| B | Block-Structured for RAG | 200-400 word self-contained blocks; comparison tables under 10 rows |
| L | Latest and Consistent | Monosemantic data across touchpoints; visible "last updated" timestamps |
| E | Entity Graph and Schema | Exact-match JSON-LD that mirrors visible text |

## How to Apply This Framework in a Report

Standard auditor workflow:

1. Score each URL against the GEO-16 pillars (binary or partial credit per pillar)
2. Calculate G as normalized score in [0, 1]
3. Identify H (count of pillars with positive hits)
4. Cross-reference each gap against CITABLE for actionable recommendations
5. Prioritize recommendations by expected effect size (see `04-effect-sizes.md`)
6. Differentiate recommendations by target engine (see `05-engine-specific.md`)
7. Calculate AI Share of Voice and Citation Rate against competitors (see `06-kpis-and-metrics.md`)
