01 / EXECUTIVE VERDICT

What the engines see, and what they don't.

Dr. Schwitzer Plastic Surgery does not appear in any of the eighteen AI-engine responses generated for this audit.

Across the four engines tested with six customer-style queries each, the brand was not named once. Not in the hyper-local prompts. Not in the procedure-specific prompts. Not even when the engine produced a list of Miami plastic surgeons. The competitive set in those answers is dominated by surgeons with high parametric memory weight: Hochstein, Salzhauer (Dr. Miami), Maercks, Mendieta. The pattern is consistent with the Evertune finding that brand search volume is the single strongest predictor of AI citation frequency1.

The brand is structurally classified as a niche player with zero AI Share of Voice. Engine confidence in the brand entity ranges from 10 percent (Perplexity) to 78 percent (Claude), which means the engines can recognize the brand if asked directly, but they will not surface it unprompted. The score ceiling on this audit was Claude at 16 / 100; the floor was Perplexity at 6 / 100. All four engines sit far below the 75 / 100 industry threshold.

The path to challenger status is sequenced and concrete. First, off-site review platform presence on RealSelf, Healthgrades, ABPS and ASPS to build the cross-document consensus that engines require before they cite a provider2. Second, an owned-site structural overhaul into RAG-compatible 200 to 400 word semantic blocks, with explicit entity restatement in every section opening3. Third, JSON-LD schema implementation with Wikidata Q-ID verified entity definitions to move citation accuracy from 62 percent to 97 percent and unlock Knowledge Graph entry4. Healthcare's low sector concentration means these moves can produce measurable AI citation share within 3 to 6 months without matching the media budgets of dominant competitors5.

Inference — operator review required

Gemini returned zero source citations across all six prompts. This is anomalous and may reflect a response-format limitation in data collection rather than true absence of citation behavior. A re-audit with adjusted Gemini prompting is recommended before drawing structural conclusions about that engine specifically.

05 / STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS

What works in the brand's favor, and what doesn't.

The brand's absence is not a function of insurmountable competitive pressure. Healthcare is a low-concentration sector and the dominant Miami names did not get there through structural domain authority alone. The gap is operational.

Working in favor

  • Geographic micro-market specificity. Bay Harbor Islands is an uncontested AI-answer niche — no competitor currently owns this geo qualifier in any engine response. First-mover structural investment can establish dominance here5.
  • Healthcare sits in the low-HHI sector tier. The brand does not need to displace legacy domain authority to earn AI citations. Structural content quality and off-site review-platform presence are sufficient entry points5.
  • The platforms that drive competitor citations — RealSelf, Healthgrades, ABPS, ASPS — are all open directories with no gatekeeping beyond credential verification. The gap is execution, not structural market barriers.

Working against

  • Off-site brand mention density is at or near zero across every measured engine. Cross-platform presence on four or more non-affiliated platforms unlocks a documented 2.8× ChatGPT citation multiplier2.
  • Owned website content is not structured for RAG retrieval. 200–400 word self-contained semantic blocks with explicit entity restatement at every section opening trigger a documented 2.3× citation multiplier3 and align with Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval requirements7.
  • JSON-LD schema implementation is absent. Standard JSON-LD raises citation accuracy from 62 percent to 94 percent. Q-ID verified implementation pushes it to 97 percent and is the prerequisite for Gemini Knowledge Graph entry4.
